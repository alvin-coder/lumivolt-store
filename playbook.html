Operations · launch playbook

$0 to first sale, without guesswork.

This is the exact sequence to run after the store is deployed. Nothing here requires money upfront: hosting is free, supplier accounts are free, payment processors charge only per transaction. The only unavoidable cost is product samples — and that's the best money you'll ever spend in this business.

Week 1 — go live properly

  • 1. Publish the storePush these files to a GitHub repo → enable Pages (main branch, root). Live in ~2 minutes at yourname.github.io/repo. No account yet? Sign up takes 2 minutes with any email.
  • 2. Register supplier accountsCJdropshipping + Zendrop first (both free, both ask only for an email). Bookmark the supplier directory — it maps each catalog item to its supplier line.
  • 3. Order samples of your first 3 SKUsRecommended starters: EchoPods Pro, LumaRing Track, LunaOne Float Lamp. Budget ~$25–40 total including shipping. This is the entire launch investment.
  • 4. Open a Stripe or PayPal business accountBoth are free to open and charge only per transaction. Stripe: stripe.com → "Start now". PayPal: paypal.com → business. You'll need ID verification; do it while samples ship.
  • 5. Time your samplesWhen each sample arrives: unbox on camera (this becomes your ad creative), inspect quality, and log actual delivery time vs. the supplier's promise. Delivery-time honesty is a competitive weapon.

Week 2 — wire up real payments

  • 6. Replace demo checkout with a payment linkFastest zero-code path: create a Stripe Payment Link for each product and point each "Add" button at it. No backend needed; Stripe handles card capture, receipts, and fraud.
  • 7. Or drop in a static checkoutFor a more integrated look: create a free Snipcart / PayPal static-site button account and swap the demo modal for their widget. Both work on pure static hosting.
  • 8. Connect the supplier appCJ's free Shopify app is the long path; the static path is their manual order form or Zendrop's fulfillment API on free tier. Each sale = one supplier PO with the customer's address.
  • 9. Set the order-status email loopStripe/PayPal send payment receipts natively. Forward the confirmation to yourself, place the supplier PO same-day, reply to the customer with the tracking number. Under 10 minutes per order at the start.

Weeks 3–4 — first traffic, first signal

  • 10. Post organic content firstZero-budget launch: unboxing + "here's what it actually does" short videos (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) using your sample footage. The gadget niche rewards demos over ads. 3 posts/product over 2 weeks.
  • 11. Only then, small paid testsIf organic shows signal (views → clicks → store visits), put $5/day behind the best performer for 3 days. Kill anything under 1% CTR. Total risk: $15.
  • 12. Fulfill fast, communicate fasterPlace each supplier PO within 24h of payment and send tracking proactively. Late tracking = disputes, and disputes are how new stores die.
  • 13. Watch the two metrics that matterGross margin per order ≥ 50% (use the margin table) and delivery time vs. promise. Everything else is noise until 10+ orders/week.

Margin math (grounded in 2026 category data)

What "demo" means — and the honest path to "real"

This store is a complete, working storefront except for three things that legally require an account holder (you):

  1. Payment capture — needs your Stripe/PayPal account (free, ~15 min, ID required).
  2. Supplier accounts — CJ/Zendrop/Spocket signups are free but must be yours, so orders and payouts bind to your identity.
  3. Live product data — the ten SKUs here are modeled on real 2026 product classes; before launch you confirm each against a live supplier quote and sample, then adjust price/copy if needed.

Everything else — catalog, cart, checkout flow, shipping rules, supplier vetting, the launch sequence — is done and in your hands.

Reality check: no supplier on earth is "100% trusted" — CJ processes millions of orders and still has bad batches. Trust is a process (sample → small orders → scale), not a platform badge. The red-flag checklist is that process, compressed.
